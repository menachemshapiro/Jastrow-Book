Type in the search box and choose “Search all text”.

Marcus Jastrow’s A Dictionary of the Targumim, the Talmud Babli and Yerushalmi, and the Midrashic Literature (London: Luzac, 1903), laid out as the printed book: every page and every column begins and ends on the same word as the 1903 edition, so a page reference to “Jastrow p. 1087” lands on the right page.

Reading

  • Spread shows two facing pages, Page one, and Text the page’s text reflowed for a small screen.
  • Turn pages with ‹ ›, the slider at the bottom, the arrow keys, or a swipe on a phone. Type a page number into the box to jump there; the preface and abbreviations use their Roman numerals (v, xv).
  • Click any entry to read it in larger type, whole even when it runs over two columns or pages. From there: the previous and next entries, A− and A+ for the type size, “Show on the page”, “Open on Sefaria”, “Copy citation”, and “Copy link”.
  • Dotted words are cross-references within the dictionary. Citations of sources (“Ber. 26b”) open the passage on Sefaria.

Finding

  • Search matches headwords as you type, in Hebrew (with or without vowel points) or by page number; “Search all text” looks through every entry, English and Hebrew.
  • א־ב opens a Hebrew keyboard, alphabetical or in the standard Israeli layout.
  • Contents lists every page by its first and last headword, letter by letter, as in the running heads of the book.
  • Citations lists the entries that cite a chosen daf of the Talmud or chapter of Onkelos. Choose בבלי or ירושלמי, the tractate and the daf. Citations are read from Jastrow’s own abbreviations: the Bavli by daf and amud (“Ber. 26b”), the Yerushalmi by chapter and the folio and column of the Krotoshin edition (“Y. Ber. VIII, 12c”); “Ib.” and a bare folio count as the tractate cited just before.
  • The same panel covers the Targumim and Midrash Rabbah. Choose the source from the menu at the top:
    • תרגום אונקלוס: “Targ. O. Gen. XVII, 4”, or simply “Targ. Gen. XVII, 4” (on the Torah, “Targ.” alone is Onkelos).
    • תרגום יונתן על התורה (Pseudo-Jonathan): Jastrow’s “Targ. Y.” and “Targ. Y. I”.
    • תרגום ירושלמי (the Fragment Targum): Jastrow’s “Targ. Y. II”.
    • תרגום יונתן על הנביאים: “Targ. Is. XXX, 2”, “Targ. I Sam. …”.
    • תרגום כתובים: Psalms, Proverbs, Job, the Megillot (with Targum Sheni to Esther, “Targ. II Esth.”) and Chronicles.
    • מדרש רבה: the Rabbah on the Torah by section (“Gen. R. s. 19”), and on the Megillot by the verse commented on (“Lam. R. to I, 5”) or the introduction (“Lam. R. introd.”).
    • משנה by tractate, chapter and mishnah (“Ber. VII, 1”, “Aboth I, 5”), and תוספתא by tractate, chapter and halakha (“Tosef. Sabb. XVI (XVII), 3”, “Tosef. Kel. B. Mets. VIII, 6”). Where Jastrow gives a second chapter number in parentheses, the first is used.
    • מדרש תהלים by psalm and verse (“Midr. Till. to Ps. XXII, 4”), פסיקתא רבתי by section (“Pesik. R. s. 18”), and ילקוט שמעוני by book and remez (“Yalk. Gen. 61”).
    Pick the book and the chapter or section; the entries are listed verse by verse. A reference that follows on from one of these (“Targ. O. ib. XLI, 43”, “Ib. 16”, “; s. 78”) counts as the same source.

Sources and thanks

  • The text of the dictionary is Sefaria’s digitization of Jastrow, as served by Sefaria in 2026. The entry ids, the links to sources and the “Open on Sefaria” links are Sefaria’s. Our thanks to Sefaria for making it freely available.
  • Corrections. We have edited the dictionary’s text where we believe the digital text has a typo, such as a missing space (“ch. same.Targ.” for “ch. same. Targ.”) or words run together (“sameIthpalp.”). We have also corrected 12 Talmud references that are misprinted in the 1903 book itself: each cites a daf that does not exist (Shabbat 182ᵃ, when Shabbat ends at 157ᵇ), and searching the Talmud for the quotation found it on the right one (152ᵃ). Some 70 more were checked one by one against the scans of the book, among them “Gem.” restored where the digital text has “Ib.”, citations given twice, and words or passages the digital text leaves out. These are our corrections, made on our own judgment; they are not Sefaria’s text and not Jastrow’s. Every one is listed, with the page, the entry, the text before and after, and the reason, in text_fixes.json, and each kind of correction is its own entry in the project’s history.
  • Column breaks, title pages and corrections were checked against scans of the 1903 printing from HebrewBooks.org. Corrections to the text are listed, each with its reason, in the project’s text_fixes.json.
  • The dictionary itself (1903) is in the public domain.
  • Source code, data and the history of every change: github.com/menachemshapiro/Jastrow-Book.
